Abstract

Older adults who manage their own medication at home make frequent errors — polypharmacy, cognitive decline, and physical limits all play a part. Most assistive technology only reminds people to take a dose; it does not check that the pills in hand are the right ones. A review of 25 papers shows that no prior study tests vision-based pill counting or verification on a real home robot. This project asks whether a tabletop social robot (Reachy Mini) with a vision-language model (VLM) can help with that verification step. We first tested three VLMs on 15 controlled pill images using three prompts; Gemini was the most accurate, with overlapping and touching pills as the main failure mode. Prompting on its own did not fix counting errors. We then built a voice-conversational verification system that scans bottles, verifies doses, sets reminders, and checks dose history — with safety rules that keep the robot inside the trust boundary older adults prefer: it verifies and reminds, but never gives clinical advice. A four-route camera test (20 trials) shows the system counts reliably except when pills are dense and overlapping — a limit of the vision task itself, not the robot's camera or code path. We show three end-to-end interactions and discuss the design, its limits, and future work.

The Project, Step by Step

  1. Literature review (25 papers). Found the gap: lots of technology reminds people to take medication, but almost none uses vision to verify the right pills on a real home robot.
  2. Experiment 1 — model comparison. ChatGPT vs. Claude vs. Gemini on 15 pill images and 3 prompts. Gemini was the most accurate; overlapping and touching pills were the hardest cases.
  3. Experiment 2 — prompting study. Gemini with 10 different prompts. Prompting alone did not reliably fix counting errors.
  4. Camera comparison test. A four-route test to isolate where counts break: it was only the dense, overlapping pill layouts — not the camera, not the code.
  5. Built the app. A voice-conversational assistant on the Reachy Mini: bottle scanning, dose verification, reminders, dose history, and safety rules.
  6. Three end-to-end demos recorded on the real robot.

Demos

  • Scan & save: read a bottle label, save the medication, and set a reminder.
  • Verify & log: verify a dose, log it, and tell whether it was already taken today.
  • Catch a mistake: catch a wrong pill count, explain it, then confirm the corrected count.
